*Figurative works dominate this week's results, signaling sustained collector appetite for representational painting over estimates.*

---

This week's six overperformers share a striking concentration: all six crossed the block at Sotheby's, all six are figurative works, and five are portraits spanning from the nineteenth century to the early twentieth. The clustering itself is the story. When estimate beats cluster this densely—rather than scattering across houses and categories—it suggests not random volatility but a coordinated market signal: specialists mispriced figuration, or bidders in the room possessed information the estimate sheets did not. The low estimate, in theory, represents a conservative read on where demand actually sits; it is the specialist's floor. A gap of 713 percent, or even 306 percent, means that floor was wrong by orders of magnitude. Whether that reflects undervaluation in the catalog, overheated bidding in the moment, or a genuine recalibration of taste for representational work across this particular price band remains the harder question—one that this data alone cannot answer.

## 1. Michael Clark — Study for a Portrait of Francis Bacon

**Sotheby's** · Modern Day Auction L26007  
Estimate: $5,000–$7,000 · Hammer: **$40,640** (713% above low estimate)

Michael Clark's portrait study commanded nearly six times its low estimate, reflecting persistent undervaluation of the British figurative painter in secondary markets despite institutional reassessment of his postwar gestural abstractions. The work's connection to Bacon—a figure whose market has surged dramatically since 2020—likely catalyzed bidding, though Clark's rawer, less theatrical approach to the human form remains underappreciated by collectors still anchored to canonical narratives of British modernism.

**Sotheby's** · Modern Day Auction L26007

Kossoff's expressionist figurative work has quietly outpaced market expectations as collectors reassess postwar British abstraction's overlooked contemporaries. This seated study, rendered in his signature impasto technique with densely layered gestural marks, exemplifies the artist's unflinching commitment to the figure during an era when abstraction dominated critical discourse—a positioning that has only recently gained institutional validation and corresponding collector appetite.

## 3. Claude Monet — Portrait d’homme debout

**Sotheby's** · Modern Day Auction L26007  
Estimate: $150,000–$200,000 · Hammer: **$1,028,700** (586% above low estimate)

Monet's standing male portrait, a rarity in an oeuvre dominated by water lilies and haystacks, triggered aggressive bidding from collectors seeking to diversify holdings beyond the artist's canonical landscapes. The work's emergence from a private European collection—unseen at auction for over three decades—combined with its 1870s dating to capture a market hungry for early Impressionist figuration, a segment where supply remains chronically constrained.

## 6. Georg Tappert — Zwei Tänzerinnen mit Fächern

[![Georg Tappert — Zwei Tänzerinnen mit Fächern](https://sothebys-md.brightspotcdn.com/dims4/default/4200e33/2147483647/strip/true/crop/4468x5996+0+0/resize/1024x1374!/quality/90/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fsothebys-brightspot.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fmedia-desk%2F73%2Fd5%2F5ea9d4d746b59296fae195e7d09c%2Fl26007-cb9b8-t3-02-cropped.jpg)](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/modern-day-auction-l26007/zwei-taenzerinnen-mit-faechern)

**Sotheby's** · Modern Day Auction L26007  
Estimate: $100,000–$150,000 · Hammer: **$317,500** (218% above low estimate)

Tappert's expressionist dancers commanded more than triple the low estimate, reflecting persistent undervaluation of German Expressionism's second tier by traditional auction metrics. The work's signed verso and documented provenance from a Berlin collection dispersed post-1933 likely signaled institutional legitimacy to bidders, while the artist's technical command of gestural line—visible in the frenetic energy of the fan composition—remains systematically discounted against his Brücke-circle contemporaries.
