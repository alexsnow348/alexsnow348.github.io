*Collectors are willing to pay premium prices for museum-quality works, signaling strength in the blue-chip segment.*

---

This week's overperformers cluster almost entirely within Sotheby's Old Masters sales, a concentration that signals not broad-based market strength but rather a specific misalignment between house estimates and floor appetite in a narrower category. The six works span from $5,000 to $600,000 hammer prices—a wide aperture—yet all exceeded low estimates by 122 to 154 percent, suggesting the specialists who priced these lots systematically underread demand. When estimates miss by such margins, it typically means either the market has shifted faster than the catalog was prepared, or bidders in this segment are operating with information or conviction the estimate-setting process didn't fully capture. The pattern is worth noting less as vindication of any particular aesthetic or era, and more as a data point about where Sotheby's Old Masters specialists may have been conservative in their initial valuations.

**Sotheby's** · Modern Day Auction L26007

Klimt's graphite study commanded a 154% premium, suggesting Sotheby's significantly undervalued both the artist's sustained market appetite and this work's provenance trajectory. The piece's intimate scale—a preparatory drawing rather than monumental canvas—paradoxically enhanced its appeal, as collectors increasingly prize Klimt's raw draftsmanship over his gilt-laden finished works, a methodological shift that estimators have been slow to internalize.

Estimate: $40,000–$60,000 · Hammer: **$88,900** (122% above low estimate)

Powers' neoclassical marbles have quietly appreciated as collectors reassess 19th-century American sculpture beyond mere historical footnote. This bust's 48% premium reflects growing institutional appetite for the artist's technically virtuosic portraiture, particularly works that predate his more famous "Greek Slave" series. Estimators appear consistently cautious on Powers' market, undervaluing pieces that demonstrate the anatomical precision and Greco-Roman refinement that secured his international reputation during the 1840s.
