*Figurative and historical works dominate this week, suggesting sustained collector appetite for narrative-driven Old Masters and academic painting.*

---

This week's overperformers cluster almost entirely within a single Sotheby's sale, with six works clearing their low estimates by 100 percent or more—a concentration that signals not broad market strength but rather a specialist's miscalibration on a particular group. The estimate, in theory, represents the auctioneer's best read on current demand; when multiple lots in the same session dramatically exceed these benchmarks, it suggests the presale valuation either lagged genuine collector appetite or underestimated the competitive intensity in that room. The works span a considerable range—from Delacroix and Chagall in the six-figure realm to Boucher and Leighton in the modest thousands—yet all punched above their assigned ceiling. Such uniform outperformance across different price tiers and media (painting, drawing) warrants scrutiny: it may reflect accurate market sentiment that the house underestimated, or it may point to a particular buyer or constituency that materialized unexpectedly on sale day.

## 1. Eugène Delacroix — Lion et lionne dans les montagnes

[![Eugène Delacroix — Lion et lionne dans les montagnes](https://sothebys-md.brightspotcdn.com/dims4/default/5f7eb18/2147483647/strip/true/crop/2000x1537+0+0/resize/1024x787!/quality/90/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fsothebys-brightspot.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fmedia-desk%2Fwebnative%2Fimages%2F63%2Fb2%2F903d2cfd4367b37061c1b70e6eb4%2Fl26040-dlb7q-t3-04.jpg)](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/master-works-on-paper-from-five-centuries-2/lion-et-lionne-dans-les-montagnes)

**Sotheby's** · Master Works On Paper From Five Centuries 2  
Estimate: $250,000–$350,000 · Hammer: **$550,000** (120% above low estimate)

Delacroix's romantic fervor continues to outpace conservative valuations, with this dynamic study of predatory tension—rendered in the artist's characteristic sepia and graphite—capturing the raw naturalism that distinguished his animal studies from academic convention. The 57% hammer premium reflects persistent collector appetite for the French master's works on paper, a category long underestimated relative to his monumental canvases.

Estimate: $15,000–$20,000 · Hammer: **$30,480** (103% above low estimate)

Leighton's bronze, cast from a model the Victorian master created in 1881, benefited from renewed institutional interest in late-nineteenth-century academic sculpture—a category long undervalued by the market. The work's meticulous surface finish and its origins in the artist's prolific late period, when he commanded the Royal Academy presidency, evidently resonated beyond the house's conservative estimate.

## 5. Marc Chagall — La Femme rouge au coq

[![Marc Chagall — La Femme rouge au coq](https://sothebys-md.brightspotcdn.com/dims4/default/7bc95e0/2147483647/strip/true/crop/1695x2000+0+0/resize/1024x1208!/quality/90/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fsothebys-brightspot.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fmedia-desk%2F45%2Fe8%2Ff255147443d6b5bcf6491818639c%2Fl26007-5ryk7-t1-02-cropped.jpg)](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/modern-day-auction-l26007/la-femme-rouge-au-coq)

**Sotheby's** · Modern Day Auction L26007  
Estimate: $300,000–$400,000 · Hammer: **$609,600** (103% above low estimate)

Chagall's poetic figurative language continues to outpace conservative market assessments, particularly as collectors reassess mid-century modernism's emotional depth against contemporary abstraction. "La Femme rouge au coq"—the rooster motif recurring throughout his oeuvre as a symbol of vitality and resurrection—demonstrated robust competition from multiple bidders, suggesting estimators systematically undervalue his narrative canvases when they emphasize recognizable symbolic content over formal innovation.
