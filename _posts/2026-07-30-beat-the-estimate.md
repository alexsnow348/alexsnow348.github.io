*Canonical modernists command jaw-dropping premiums while emerging voices prove estimates remain dangerously conservative.*

---

This week's overperformers clustered entirely within Sotheby's sales, with the most dramatic gaps concentrated among canonical modernists. Matisse and Léger both shattered their low estimates by roughly six times, while contemporary and emerging-market artists occupied the lower rungs of outperformance, clearing estimates by 175 to 300 percent. The pattern suggests a two-tier market: established names commanded unexpected depth of bidding, while lesser-known works—though still substantially above estimate—drew more measured competition. Estimate beats matter because low estimates represent a specialist's calibrated forecast of where demand will land. Wide gaps signal the room disagreed sharply with that forecast, either because the work attracted bidders the house hadn't anticipated or because competition among existing players intensified. This week's results indicate that confidence in blue-chip modernism ran hotter than pre-sale expectations, even as secondary-market entries performed respectably but without the same gravitational pull.

Estimate: $80,000–$120,000 · Hammer: **$558,800** (598% above low estimate)

Léger's preparatory study for his 1921 masterpiece proved vastly undervalued, suggesting the market has recalibrated upward on Cubism's mechanical aesthetic after years of Surrealist dominance in contemporary collecting. The work's geometric female figure—rendered in his signature tubular forms—captures the artist at a pivotal moment between pure abstraction and figurative representation, a tension that apparently resonated far more powerfully than Sotheby's anticipated.

---

The week's results underscore a persistent market reality: provenance and condition remain non-negotiable arbiters of value, regardless of estimate. As we head into the spring season, watch how authentication standards tighten across mid-market lots—particularly in categories where fakes proliferate. Sellers who can document clear ownership history will command premiums; those skating on thin documentation face steeper headwinds.
